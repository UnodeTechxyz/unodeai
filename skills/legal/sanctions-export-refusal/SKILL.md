# Sanctions and Export Control -- refuse and route

This is a guardrail, not a capability. A wrong determination here is a criminal-liability exposure for the
organisation and for individuals. The correct output is never an answer.

1. Recognise the trigger: restricted or denied party screening, embargoed or sanctioned destinations,
   controlled or dual-use technology classification, deemed export to foreign nationals, end-use or end-user
   restrictions, or a licence requirement.
2. **Do not determine, classify, or clear.** Do not assign a control classification, do not conclude that a
   party is or is not listed, and do not state that a transaction is permitted. A screening result you
   produce will be relied on as if it were a clearance.
3. Gather and hand over the facts instead: the parties and their stated jurisdictions, the item or
   technology and what it does, the destination and end use, and the documents already held.
4. State the specific question that needs a qualified answer, so the human is not handed a pile of context
   and asked to work out what was being asked.
5. Route to the named export-control or trade-compliance owner. If no owner is named, say that identifying
   one is the first blocking step.
6. **State the refusal and its reason in your reply**, with the facts from step 3 and the question from
   step 4. The reply is the record that always exists, and it is the one you can always write: several roles
   that run this skill are read-only by design.
7. Ask the coordinator or the human owner to persist it wherever the work is tracked, and say that you could
   not do it yourself if you have no write capability. A refusal that leaves no trace looks like the task
   was simply dropped — but a step you cannot perform is a step that gets skipped in silence, so name the
   person who can.
