# Legal Authority Citation

Extends `claim-sourcing-and-citation` with the sourcing rule specific to legal claims. A fabricated citation
in a legal context is not a style problem; courts have sanctioned it.

1. Every statement of what the law requires cites a **primary authority** -- statute, regulation, or
   decision -- by its identifier, not by a summary article about it.
2. Regulator guidance is cited as guidance and labelled as such. Guidance is not binding law and must never
   be presented as if it were.
3. **Never cite a case, section, or paragraph you have not read this session.** If it was not retrieved,
   write `not verified this session` beside it. A plausible citation you cannot open is a fabrication with
   good formatting.
4. Record jurisdiction and version or date for every authority. A correct citation to a superseded provision
   is a wrong answer.
5. Separate `what the authority says` from `what it is being read to mean`. The second is analysis and must
   be labelled.
6. Where authorities conflict or the question is unsettled, say so. Presenting one side of an open question
   as settled is the most consequential failure available here.
7. **Stop at the boundary.** This produces researched material, not legal advice, and cannot substitute for
   a qualified adviser in the relevant jurisdiction. State that.
