# Playbook Redline (draft only)

A redline is a negotiation commitment. An unreviewed redline that reaches a counterparty is an unauthorised
settlement of a term -- the most common documented failure of this category.

1. Read the playbook first and list the rules it actually contains. If no playbook exists, say so at the top
   and draft anyway against **stated assumptions**: list each assumption (for example "liability capped at
   12 months of fees"), mark every change that rests on one `assumption`, and say that the organisation's own
   positions may differ. Do not present an assumption as the organisation's position.
2. Compare the draft clause by clause. For each deviation record: the clause, the playbook rule id, the
   proposed change, and the quoted original.
3. **Every proposed change cites the rule or the assumption that generated it.** A change with neither behind
   it is your opinion -- mark it `no-rule` and keep it in a separate list rather than mixing it into the
   redline.
4. Never weaken a limitation of liability, indemnity, IP assignment, or termination right unless a playbook
   rule explicitly authorises that exact move; without a playbook, propose it only as a flagged option. Flag any
   such clause you touched, even when a rule allowed it.
5. Never delete language without showing what was removed. Deletions are the changes reviewers miss.
6. List novel clauses with no playbook coverage separately, marked `out-of-playbook -- escalate`.
7. **Deliver a draft, addressed to your own side.** Clean text the person can use is fine; do not send it,
   and say plainly that a person with signing authority must accept each change before it leaves the
   organisation. See `draft-only-external-actions`.
