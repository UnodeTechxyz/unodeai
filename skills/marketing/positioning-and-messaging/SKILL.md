# Positioning and Messaging

1. Identify the primary audience, the problem they already recognize, and the context in which it is
   urgent.
2. State the category and the differentiated outcome in one plain-language positioning sentence.
3. Choose two or three proof points that can be demonstrated honestly. Do not lead with unsupported
   superlatives or competitor claims.
4. Translate the position into a message hierarchy: headline, supporting value, proof, and one clear
   call to action.
5. Adapt the wording for the audience and channel while preserving the same core promise and limits.

Return: positioning statement, message hierarchy, proof requirements, and channel-specific variants.
